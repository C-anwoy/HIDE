\section{Conclusions}
\revisionr3{We introduced HIDE, a training-free score computed from selected input/output hidden states during a single generation. The original benchmark evaluates its detection performance against uncertainty-based and multi-generation methods. The new matched comparison strengthens the baseline coverage but narrows the conclusions: attention mass is stronger in seven of eight settings, HIDE is stronger on Gemma/RACE, and norm behavior depends on model and dataset. A selected-token-count control nearly reproduces the configured HIDE score, so these results do not establish that nonlinear dependence is its principal predictive mechanism. Paired standard-inference timings extending to Gemma-2-27B demonstrate feasibility at the tested scales, with variable overhead and no optimized-serving guarantee.}

\section*{Limitations}
\revisionr3{The adapted score depends strongly on the realized selected-token count in the corrected experiments. For all-ones input and output Gram matrices, its value is $(n-1)/n^2$ for $n\geq1$, rather than zero in general. The empirical count-control correlations and AUC differences therefore limit interpreting the score as direct evidence of semantic dependence. Very short answers and the zero fallback require particular care.}

\revisionr3{The original tables retain the earlier generation and evaluation protocol. The stopping implementation could continue beyond an answer, altering correctness labels and representation scores; the corrected first-answer-line comparison is reported separately and does not retroactively validate those full-benchmark values. It covers two models and a fixed 2,000-example subset per dataset, selected for the revision deadline after earlier results were examined. No examples were selected by outcome, but this is not a preregistered evaluation. Reference-similarity thresholds are imperfect correctness proxies, and only the first reference is used.}

\revisionr3{HIDE requires white-box hidden-state access, and the attention baseline requires attention-weight access. The norm baseline is an unprojected proxy, not the actual residual update. Observational correlations and QCBW subsets do not identify causal mechanisms. The retained timing suite uses the benchmark stopping protocol on a shared host; it does not measure optimized serving or the new stopping criterion's overhead. Hidden-width coverage remains limited despite adding a larger-parameter model. Claims about semantic alignment, architectural scaling, or deployment latency should be understood within these limits.}
