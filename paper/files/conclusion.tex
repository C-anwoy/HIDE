\section{Conclusions}
\revisionr3{We introduced HIDE, a training-free detector computed from selected input and output hidden states during one generation. Across six models and four QA datasets, it is competitive with the evaluated baselines. It reduces mean latency by $50.8\%$ relative to five-generation EigenScore under standard HuggingFace inference. Attention performs better in seven of eight directly compared settings; HIDE performs better on Gemma-2-9B/RACE. The selected-token-count control closely tracks HIDE, limiting a nonlinear-dependence explanation of its performance. Timing measurements up to Gemma-2-27B show that the added overhead varies with model and dataset. These results show where HIDE is useful and where simpler signals perform better.}

\section*{Limitations}
\revisionr3{The adapted score depends strongly on the number of selected tokens. For all-ones input and output Gram matrices, it equals $(n-1)/n^2$ for $n\geq1$, rather than zero in general. Its agreement with this control limits a semantic-dependence interpretation.  The assumptions of the asymptotic result in Appendix A.3 need not hold for selected tokens within a single answer.}

\revisionr3{The full-benchmark tables use stopping conditions that can allow generation to continue beyond an answer. This can affect correctness labels and scores. The first-answer-line comparison avoids this source of variation, but covers only two models and 2,000 examples per dataset. Its sample size was chosen after earlier runs had been inspected, although examples were not selected by outcome. It does not validate the full-benchmark results under these stopping conditions. Sentence similarity and ROUGE-L are imperfect measures of correctness, and the evaluation uses only the first reference answer.}

\revisionr3{HIDE requires access to hidden states; the attention baseline requires attention weights. The norm baseline omits value and output projections and is therefore only a proxy for the input-derived update. Correlations and the QCBW comparison cannot identify causal mechanisms. The timing experiments use standard inference and benchmark stopping conditions; they do not measure optimized serving or the first-answer-line protocol. Host sharing may affect the measurements, and the range of hidden widths is limited even with Gemma-2-27B.}
