\begin{abstract}
\revisionr3{Contemporary language models can generate fluent responses that are factually incorrect or unfaithful to the input context. We propose HIDE, a training-free, single-generation hallucination detector that applies an adapted HSIC-based score to selected input and output hidden states, without requiring attention weights. We evaluate six open-source language models on four QA benchmarks covering faithfulness and factuality. Across the main benchmark, HIDE achieves approximately $29\%$ mean relative AUC improvement over the better of the evaluated perplexity and energy baselines within each model/dataset setting, and is competitive with multi-generation methods. Under standard HuggingFace inference, its mean latency reduction relative to the five-generation EigenScore baseline is $50.8\%$. Additional paired timing measurements extend to Gemma-2-27B and show model- and dataset-dependent overhead. A matched 2,000-example comparison identifies the scope of the detection advantage: attention mass is stronger in seven of eight settings, while HIDE is stronger on Gemma/RACE. A selected-token-count control limits a specifically nonlinear-dependence interpretation of the configured score. Together, these results characterize the performance, computational trade-offs, and limitations of a hidden-state-based, single-generation detector.}
\footnote{\revisionr3{Code and retained experimental records: \url{https://github.com/C-anwoy/HIDE}.}}
\end{abstract}
