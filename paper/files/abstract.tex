\begin{abstract}
\revisionr3{Contemporary language models can generate fluent responses that are factually incorrect or unfaithful to the input context. We propose HIDE, a training-free, single-pass hallucination detector that applies an adapted Hilbert--Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) score to selected input and output hidden states. We evaluate six open-source language models on four QA benchmarks covering faithfulness and factuality. Across the main benchmark, HIDE achieves approximately $29\%$ mean relative AUC improvement over the better of the perplexity and energy baselines in each model/dataset setting, and is competitive with multi-pass methods. Under standard HuggingFace inference, its mean latency reduction relative to five-generation EigenScore is $50.8\%$. Timing measurements extending to Gemma-2-27B show that overhead varies across models and datasets. A separate comparison on the same 2,000 answers per setting finds that attention mass performs better in seven of eight settings, while HIDE performs better on Gemma-2-9B/RACE. A control based only on the number of selected tokens closely tracks HIDE, limiting the interpretation of its performance as evidence of nonlinear dependence. These results establish the strengths and limitations of HIDE as a detector that uses hidden states without requiring attention weights.}
\footnote{\revisionr3{Code and retained experimental records: \url{https://github.com/C-anwoy/HIDE}.}}
\end{abstract}
