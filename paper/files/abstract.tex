\begin{abstract}
\changed{
Contemporary Language Models (LMs) often generate responses that are fluent yet factually incorrect or unfaithful to the input context, a phenomenon commonly referred to as hallucination. Existing hallucination detection methods frequently rely on multiple generations per input, resulting in increased computational cost and inference latency.
In this work, we propose a training-free, single-pass method for \textbf{H}allucination detect\textbf{I}on via \textbf{D}ecoupled r\textbf{E}presentations (\textit{\texttt{\textbf{HIDE}}}). HIDE is motivated by the hypothesis that hallucinations arise when an LM’s internal representations of the input context become statistically decoupled from those of the generated output. We quantify this decoupling using the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) computed over hidden-state representations extracted during generation.
We evaluate HIDE on four question answering benchmarks, covering both faithfulness and factuality hallucinations, across six open-source LMs of varying scales. Across models and datasets, HIDE outperforms existing single-pass baselines, achieving an average relative improvement of approximately $29\%$ in AUC-ROC. Moreover, HIDE remains competitive with, and often outperforms, multi-pass state-of-the-art methods, while \changedtwo{providing a baseline reduction in computation time of approximately 51\% under standard inference}. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of exploiting internal representation decoupling in LMs for efficient and practical hallucination detection.
}\footnote{The code to reproduce our results is open-sourced: \url{https://github.com/C-anwoy/HIDE}.}
\end{abstract}
